02From problem to design direction

Credible and organized, but unmistakably youth-led — not corporate.

The existing site was the starting point. The redesign focused on visual hierarchy, clarity of programs and calls to action, consistency across pages, and communication of the organization's youth-led identity.

The site needed to work for several audiences at once — students, mentors, partners, donors, and parents. Visual and interaction decisions were built around the organization's mission and personality: clear program entry points, visible calls to action, student-centered messaging, and a more recognizable visual identity.

Initial direction → feedback → refined direction

I met regularly with the board and technical team and iterated on the design based on feedback from the founder and other members. My initial direction was selected, then refined around messaging, navigation, hierarchy, program visibility, and implementation feasibility.

03From coded prototype to Webflow

A prototype you can click beats a mockup you have to imagine.

After establishing the visual direction, I used AI-assisted coding to build a functional HTML/CSS/JavaScript version of the site. Rather than relying only on static mockups, the coded prototype let layout, interactions, and page behavior be tested directly in the browser — and gave the team something concrete to review during feedback.

AI was a development assistant, not the designer. I directed the visual system, layout, interaction goals, page structure, and every revision.

Translating the prototype into Webflow

Once the direction was established, I rebuilt the prototype in Webflow so the organization could maintain and edit the site themselves. Transferring a coded interface into Webflow meant constantly reading properties — parent containers, inherited styles, dimensions, spacing, typography, and layout behavior.
